\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \scl{}, a training-free layer between an unchanged detector and an unchanged
tracker. Nested Otsu splits of recent logits decide whether the scores are clean or noisy; on clean
scores the layer keeps the tracker's settings, on noisy scores it chooses the candidates, rescores
them, and sets the thresholds, and a continuation rescue gives single-stage trackers the step they
lack. One frozen configuration, tested on seven published trackers, four detection sources, and
three datasets, restored two trackers from 0 to about 66 HOTA under halved scores, raised the HOTA
of both single-stage trackers on every MOT17 sequence, raised the MOTA of two-stage trackers by up to 7.86 points
on KITTI and 24.70 points on held-out aerial data while cutting identity switches by up to 56\%, and
reached higher HOTA than simple adaptive thresholds and the published ACT method. It kept well-matched trackers
at their own accuracy, showed no statistically significant HOTA loss in any tested cell, and took
2.95 ms per frame on a CPU. The score scale of the detector should therefore be treated as part of
the tracking system. Future work will test the layer with more detectors, trackers, and datasets.
