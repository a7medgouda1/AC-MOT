\title[Self-Calibrating Control Layer for Multi-Object Trackers]{Training-Free Self-Calibrating Control Layer for Heterogeneous Multi-Object Trackers}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Ahmed Gouda} \sur{Ismail}}\email{a7medgouda1@gmail.com}
\author[1]{\fnm{Mohamed S.} \sur{Mohamed}}\email{mohamedms@mtc.edu.eg}
\author[2]{\fnm{Tarek Ahmed} \sur{Mahmoud}}\email{tarek.mohamed@eui.edu.eg}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Computer Engineering and Artificial Intelligence Department}, \orgname{Military Technical College}, \orgaddress{\city{Cairo}, \country{Egypt}}}
\affil[2]{\orgdiv{Computer Engineering Department}, \orgname{Egypt University of Informatics}, \orgaddress{\city{Cairo}, \country{Egypt}}}

\abstract{Many multi-object tracking systems combine a detector and a tracker that were developed separately. When the detector is replaced, retrained, or quantized for embedded hardware, its confidence scores change, but the tracker keeps thresholds that were set for another detector. This paper presents a training-free self-calibrating control layer (SCL) that sits between a frozen detector and a frozen tracker. It calibrates itself while running: it splits the detector scores of recent frames with an automatic histogram threshold (Otsu's method) and decides whether the scores are clean or noisy. On clean scores it keeps the tracker's own settings. On noisy scores it chooses which candidates reach the tracker, assigns them new scores, and sets the tracker's thresholds. It also adds a continuation step for trackers that lack one. It needs no labels and uses no future frames. One frozen configuration was tested on seven published trackers, four detection sources, and two datasets, with paired bootstrap intervals. It raised the higher order tracking accuracy (HOTA) of two single-stage trackers by 0.61 points, with intervals above zero: OC-SORT, used during development, and PD-SORT, which was not. When all detector scores were multiplied by 0.5, two trackers found no detection above their thresholds and scored 0 HOTA; with SCL, both reached about 66 HOTA. With a noisy RT-DETR-L detector, it raised the multi-object tracking accuracy (MOTA) of ByteTrack and BoT-SORT by 6.0 and 7.9 points. On four trackers with a low-score step and well-matched detections, HOTA changed by at most 0.06 points. \scl{} takes 2.95 ms per frame on a CPU without a GPU, so it adds very little delay and is suitable for real-time use.}

\keywords{Multi-object tracking, Tracking by detection, Detector confidence calibration, Online adaptation, Otsu thresholding, Real-time image processing}
