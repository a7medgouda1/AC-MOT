| 6 | Chapter 5, Section 5.2 (Future Work) | The results suggest several directions for further work: • Wider validation of AC-MOT. Testing the controller with more detectors, trackers, and aerial video datasets, and with operating points beyond the current grid of input sizes, NMS values, and score thresholds. • End-to-end trackers. Extending SCL to trackers that learn detection and association jointly and keep their thresholds inside the network, which the host contract does not yet cover. • Monitoring of detector updates. Using the label-free mode and the confident share of SCL as a running check when the detector of a deployed system is updated, retrained, or compressed. • A combined pipeline. Using both controllers in one pipeline, with AC-MOT setting the detector operating point and SCL adapting the tracker input to the resulting scores, and evaluating their interaction. • Onboard deployment. Measuring both controllers on the low-power processors used on board UAVs, where the cost of the detector input matters most. | The training-free and modular design of the two controllers opens several directions that build on these results: • New detectors and aerial platforms. Because AC-MOT needs no training and changes only three arguments of the detector call, the same calibration procedure can be applied to new detector families and aerial datasets, and its action map can include further operating points. • End-to-end trackers. The host contract of SCL can be extended to trackers that learn detection and association jointly, so that the same layer also serves this family of trackers. • Monitoring of detector updates. The label-free mode and the confident share of SCL can serve as a running check when the detector of a deployed system is updated, retrained, or compressed. • A combined pipeline. Since the two controllers act on different parts of the pipeline, they can run in one pipeline, in which AC-MOT sets the detector operating point and SCL adapts the tracker input to the resulting scores, to combine the gains of both. • Onboard deployment. Both controllers add only a few milliseconds per frame, which makes an implementation on the processors used on board UAVs a natural next step. |
